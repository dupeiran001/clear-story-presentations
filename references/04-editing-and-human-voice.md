# 04 — Ruthless editing and genuinely human voice

**Main rule:** remove the *needless*, not the *necessary*. Don't equate “fewest words” with clarity; a small worked example may save an audience minutes of confusion.

## The content filter

Classify each element as:

- **MUST SEE:** evidence, focal visual, central assertion or decision required *now*.
- **MUST HEAR:** explanation, caveat or transition best spoken instead of printed.
- **MUST HAVE AVAILABLE:** detailed assumptions, citations, derivations, backup/appendix.
- **CAN CUT:** repeated framing, show-off detail, generic claims, irrelevant background, decorative elements.

Before designing a slide, write an **invisible deletion draft** with only the takeaway, strongest evidence, and the minimum prerequisites. Build back only what serves comprehension.

## Prioritization method

For a proposed point, score qualitatively:

`Decision relevance × explanatory value × evidence strength ÷ attention cost`.

This is a **thinking heuristic**, not numerical science. A long item with high explanatory value may be indispensable; a short item can still be noise.

## Compressing without mangling

- Replace abstract noun sequences with verbs (“an optimization of the deployment process” → “we shortened deployment”).
- Replace vague magnitudes with grounded ones (“massively improved” → “latency dropped from X to Y under [test condition]”). Never invent X/Y.
- Use only the baseline needed for an inference; retain sample size, time window and uncertainty when consequential.
- Put definitions close to first use, not in an unmemorable terminology dump.
- Turn enumerations into a comparison, sequence, grouping, example, or diagram *if* relationships exist; a list is fine when enumeration itself is the task.
- Cut duplicated headings/footers and formalities if they add no navigational value.

## Distinguish four on-screen sentences

1. **Topic:** “Revenue.” (rarely helps)
2. **Observation:** “Enterprise revenue increased 12% in Q2.”
3. **Interpretation:** “Expansion in existing accounts drove most of the increase.”
4. **Decision:** “Prioritize expansion motions over new logo spend next quarter.”

A slide title should reflect what the slide *actually substantiates*. If evidence proves only (2), do not write (4) as fact. Label (4) as a recommendation.

## Human-sounding speech

Use the speaker's real vocabulary, professional register, point of view and relationship to the audience. Vary sentence lengths naturally. Speak about tangible objects and situations. Allow an honest “we don't know yet.”

**Remove stock AI / consulting filler:**

- “In today's fast-paced/evolving landscape ...”
- “Harness the power of ...”, “unlock opportunities”, “synergy”, “seamlessly”
- “Delve into”, “game-changer”, “revolutionize”, “cutting-edge”, “holistic”
- “It's important to note that ...”, “As we can clearly see ...”
- Grandly metaphorical endings or moral-of-the-story slogans.
- Repeated symmetry: “The challenge. The solution. The impact.” on every slide.

Instead use domain specifics: “The unusual part is the weekend spike.”, “That result surprised us.”, “We tried the simpler version first; it failed at the edge case.” Such lines are appropriate only if true.

## Anti-AI copy examples

**Synthetic:** “Leveraging cutting-edge innovation, our transformative platform unlocks unparalleled efficiency.”

**Grounded:** “The new routing policy removes one manual handoff. In last week's dry run, it reduced median processing time from [verified baseline] to [verified result].”

**Synthetic transition:** “Now that we've explored the fascinating landscape of challenges, let us embark upon our innovative solution.”

**Grounded transition:** “The error occurs before the database call. That points us to the queue, not the storage layer.”

**Synthetic conclusion:** “Together, we can shape a brighter, more sustainable future.”

**Grounded conclusion:** “We can reduce failure risk, but only if the 48-hour canary confirms the queue behavior.”

## Editorial review sequence

1. **Truth pass** — verify source and scope; no invented names, sources, dates, units.
2. **Audience pass** — remove assumed knowledge or irrelevant explanations.
3. **Logic pass** — each “therefore” actually follows.
4. **Deletion pass** — cut the nonessential, including attractive filler images.
5. **Oral pass** — read aloud; remove page-language, overly neat triads and strained transitions.
6. **Distinctiveness pass** — does this sound and look like *this specific subject/person*, or could it be pasted into any other deck?

## Density is not the same as length

A 25-slide live talk with one clear proof per slide may be cognitively lighter than ten slides each showing five points. Optimize **mental work per moment**, not slide count alone. Treat 6×6, 10/20/30 and 1 minute per slide as context-dependent heuristics, never rigid laws.
